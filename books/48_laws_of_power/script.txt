The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene in two minutes.

Most people believe that if you just work hard and play nice, virtue will protect you.
Here is the brutal truth.
Virtue without strategy gets you crushed.
Power is like gravity. You don't have to like it, but if you ignore its laws, you fall.

Take Law Number One. Never outshine the master.
Nicolas Fouquet was the brilliant French finance minister who built a magnificent palace and threw the most lavish party in history to impress King Louis the fourteenth.
The result? The King felt outshone, humiliated, and threw Fouquet into a dark dungeon for the rest of his life.
Always make those above you appear brilliant, and keep your own brilliance concealed.

Next, Law Number Three. Conceal your intentions.
If people know what you are aiming for, they can build defenses, lay traps, or sabotage your path.
Use decoy goals and smoke screens. Let them watch your left hand while your right hand strikes.

Then, Law Number Four. Always say less than necessary.
Powerful people impress and intimidate by saying very little.
The more you speak, the more common you appear, and the higher the risk of uttering something foolish.
Silence makes people uncomfortable, forcing them to fill the void and reveal their own hidden secrets.

Next, Law Number Nine. Win through actions, never through argument.
A verbal debate victory is hollow and breeds bitter resentment that lasts for years.
Never argue. Demonstrate.
When you show results without arguing, you win without making enemies.

Then, Law Number 28. Enter action with boldness.
Timidity creates hesitation and invites disaster.
If you are unsure of a course of action, do not attempt it.
Audacity strikes fear into your rivals, and everyone admires the bold.

When conflict is unavoidable, remember Law Number 15. Crush your enemy totally.
A viper only half-crushed will heal, hide, and strike back with deadlier venom.
If you must defeat a rival, leave zero room for retaliation.

And finally, Law 48. Assume formlessness.
Rigid plans shatter under pressure. Fixed strategies become predictable targets.
Be like water, flowing around obstacles, unpredictable and impossible to pin down.
