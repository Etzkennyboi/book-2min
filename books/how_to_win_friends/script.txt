How to Win Friends and Influence People by Dale Carnegie in two minutes.

Most people believe human beings are creatures of logic.

In reality, we are creatures of emotion,

driven by pride and ego.

If you want to gather honey, don't kick over the beehive.

Rule Number One: Never criticize, condemn, or complain.

Criticism puts people on the defensive and wounds their pride.

Even notorious criminals justify their actions.

If a killer believes he has a tender heart,

ordinary people will never blame themselves.

Rule Number Two: Give honest and sincere appreciation.

The deepest craving in human nature is the desire to feel important.

Flattery is fake, but sincere praise is life-changing.

Praise the slightest improvement, and be lavish in your encouragement.

Rule Number Three: Spark in the other person an eager want.

When you go fishing, you don't bait the hook with strawberries and cream.

You bait it with worms.

Stop talking about what you want.

Talk about what they want, and show them how to get it.

Rule Number Four: Become genuinely interested in other people.

You can make more friends in two months by caring about others

than you can in two years trying to get people interested in you.

Rule Number Five: Smile.

An action speaks louder than words,

and a genuine smile says: I like you, you make me happy.

Rule Number Six: Remember that a person's name

is to that person the sweetest sound in any language.

Andrew Carnegie built a steel empire because he remembered names

and named factories after his partners.

Rule Number Seven: Be a great listener.

Encourage others to talk about themselves and their achievements.

Rule Number Eight: The only way to get the best of an argument is to avoid it.

You cannot win an argument with words.

If you lose, you lose.

And if you win, you crush their pride and create an enemy.

Show respect for their opinion,

and never say you are wrong.

And Rule Number Nine: Let the other person feel the idea is theirs.

People support what they help create.

So tell me in the comments below.

Would you rather win every debate with cold logic

and walk alone in a room of bitter enemies?

Or stay humble, listen deeply,

and build an unbreakable circle of loyal lifelong allies?
