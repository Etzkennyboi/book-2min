Rich Dad Poor Dad by Robert Kiyosaki in two minutes.
Why do most people work hard their entire lives, earn good salaries, and still end up broke?
Schools teach you how to work for money, but never teach you how to make money work for you.
Growing up, Robert had two fathers who gave him completely opposite financial advice.
His real father, the Poor Dad, was highly educated, holding advanced degrees and a secure government job.
Yet he struggled with debt his entire life, believing that the love of money was the root of all evil.
His best friend's father, the Rich Dad, was an entrepreneur who never even finished eighth grade.
Yet he became one of the richest men in Hawaii, believing that financial ignorance was the real danger.
Kiyosaki boils wealth down to one simple, unshakeable rule.
The rich acquire assets, while the poor and middle class acquire liabilities that they think are assets.
An asset is anything that puts money directly into your pocket without you working for it.
Rental real estate, dividend paying stocks, royalties, and automated businesses.
A liability is anything that takes money out of your pocket every single month.
Car loans, credit card balances, and high interest consumer debt.
Even your personal house is a liability, draining cash through mortgages, taxes, and endless maintenance.
Here is where most people get trapped. When they get a pay raise, their spending increases with it.
They buy a bigger house, lease a flashier car, and accumulate heavier monthly expenses.
Kiyosaki calls this the Rat Race: running faster on an endless financial treadmill,
working harder and harder just to pay off the liabilities of last month.
Two powerful emotions keep people trapped on this treadmill: fear and greed.
Fear of having no money forces them to wake up and work at a job they hate.
And greed whispers that a bigger paycheck will finally solve their financial anxiety.
Giving more money to someone without financial literacy just creates bigger debts.
The wealthy break out of the Rat Race by focusing obsessively on cash flow.
Instead of spending their salary on luxury status symbols, they pour it into cash generating assets.
Those assets produce monthly income, which is immediately reinvested into even more assets.
Eventually, the passive income generated by their assets completely covers their living expenses.
At that exact moment, you achieve true financial freedom. You work because you choose to, not because you have to.
The core of Rich Dad Poor Dad is not getting rich quick; it is a fundamental shift in vocabulary.
The poor and middle class say: I cannot afford it.
The moment you say you cannot afford something, your brain shuts down and stops thinking.
The rich man asks: How can I afford it?
When you ask how you can afford it, your brain is forced to solve problems and spot hidden opportunities.
Do not work for money; work to learn high income skills, sales, and investing.

or will you build assets and make money work for you?
