The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck by Mark Manson in two minutes.

Our culture tells you that the key to a good life

is a bigger house, more followers,

and positive vibes 24/7.

But here is the brutal truth.

The desire for a more positive experience is itself a negative experience.

And accepting your struggle is a positive experience.

Meet Charles Bukowski.

A heavy drinker and rejected writer.

His tombstone reads two simple words: Don't Try.

Bukowski didn't succeed because he tried to be positive.

He succeeded because he accepted his flaws

and didn't care about societal expectations.

This brings us to the Feedback Loop from Hell.

You feel anxious, then you get anxious about being anxious.

You feel lonely, and then you beat yourself up for being lonely.

Society tricked you into believing that normal human pain is a failure.

The secret is simple. You only have a limited amount of energy to give in this life.

If you spend it caring about rude strangers,

scratched shoes, or other people's vacation photos,

you will have zero energy left for what truly matters.

True freedom is not caring about everything.

True freedom is caring only about what aligns with your core values.

Happiness is not a magical destination where all problems disappear.

Happiness is a problem-solving process.

When you solve one problem, a new problem takes its place.

True fulfillment comes from choosing problems you actually enjoy solving.

Everyone wants the trophy, but nobody wants the training.

Everyone wants to be the CEO, but nobody wants the eighty-hour workweeks.

So stop asking what you want out of life.

Ask yourself: what pain am I willing to suffer for?

You are not special.

The belief that you are extraordinary is a toxic trap that leads to entitlement.

True greatness begins when you accept that you are average

and get to work.

You cannot control every bad thing that happens to you.

But you are always one hundred percent responsible for how you respond.

And remember, death is the ultimate reality check.

In the face of your mortality, the petty opinions of strangers vanish into dust.

So tell me in the comments below.

Would you rather stay trapped in the feedback loop,

exhausting yourself trying to please everyone?

Or embrace your struggles, let go of the trivial,

and fight only for what truly matters?
