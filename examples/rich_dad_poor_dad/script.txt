Rich Dad Poor Dad by Robert Kiyosaki in two minutes.

Why do most people work hard their entire lives, earn good salaries, and still end up financially trapped?

The conventional answer is that schools don't teach us how to make money. But Kiyosaki argues the problem is deeper: most people don't know the difference between working for money and having money work for them.

Growing up, Robert had two fathers who gave him completely opposite advice.

His real father—the Poor Dad—was highly educated, held a secure government job, and believed that loving money was the root of all evil. His advice was safe: study hard, get good grades, and find a stable job.

His best friend's father—the Rich Dad—was an entrepreneur who never finished eighth grade. His philosophy was simple: money is a form of power, and financial ignorance is the real danger.

Kiyosaki boils wealth down to one simple rule: The rich buy assets, while the poor and middle class buy liabilities that they think are assets.

An asset is anything that puts money into your pocket without you working for it—like rental properties, dividend stocks, or automated businesses.

A liability is anything that takes money out of your pocket—like car loans, credit card balances, and even your personal home, which drains money every month through mortgage interest, property taxes, and maintenance.

When the average person gets a raise, their spending increases with it. They buy a bigger house, take a loan for a new car, and accumulate lifestyle debt.

Kiyosaki calls this The Rat Race: running frantically on an endless financial treadmill, working longer hours just to pay off the liabilities of last month.

Fear and greed keep people trapped. Fear of having no money pushes them to work harder, and greed makes them spend every dollar they earn.

The wealthy break out of this cycle by focusing on cash flow. Instead of using their income to fund luxury, they use it to purchase income-generating assets. Over time, the income generated by those assets pays for their living expenses and eventually funds their luxuries.

The core of Rich Dad Poor Dad is not about getting rich overnight; it is a fundamental mindset shift.

The poor and middle class say: "I can't afford it."
The rich ask: "How can I afford it?"

Because the moment you say you can't afford something, your brain stops thinking. But when you ask how you can, your mind goes to work.
